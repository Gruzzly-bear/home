Backyard Food Forest Master Guide

South-Central Pennsylvania Climate | 100% Native & Self-Sustaining

The Philosophy: Why & Where to Plant

A food forest mimics a natural woodland edge, stacking plants vertically so they don't compete for light. Instead of weeding and fertilizing a traditional garden, you build a self-feeding ecosystem.

☀️ Full Sun Zones (South/West Exposure)

Best for large fruit trees, berry shrubs, and heavy producers like hazelnuts, sunchokes, and annual patch crops that need 6-8 hours of direct light.

🌳 Dappled Shade / Understory

Best for shade-lovers like Pawpaws, Serviceberries, Ramps, and ferns that naturally grow beneath the canopy of larger trees.

🌳 Expanded Native Plant Directory

1. The Canopy (Large Trees)

  • American Persimmon: Rich, sweet fruit in late fall. Rule: Must be dead ripe (wrinkly) before eating, or it causes severe mouth puckering.
  • Shagbark Hickory: Incredible native nut tree producing sweet, pecan-like nuts. Provides peeling bark habitat for local bats.
  • Black Walnut: Massive canopy tree. Warning: Produces juglone, which inhibits growth in tomatoes and nightshades—plant away from the main garden.

2. The Understory (Smaller Trees)

  • The Pawpaw: America's largest native fruit; tastes like banana-mango. Rule: You must plant at least 2 genetically different trees for cross-pollination.
  • Serviceberry (Juneberry): Heavy yields of small, sweet, blueberry-like fruits in early summer. Loves woodland edges.
  • Red Mulberry: Fast-growing native variety (avoid invasive Asian White Mulberries). Drops massive quantities of sweet berries.

3. The Shrub Layer

  • American Hazelnut: Tough, shade-tolerant bush producing sweet nuts in late summer. Great structural mid-layer.
  • American Elderberry: Vigorous grower in damp soils. Berries must be cooked; legendary for immune-boosting syrups and jams.
  • Black Raspberry: Incredible flavor. Naturally forms a thick, defensive thorny barrier for privacy along property lines.
  • Highbush Blueberry: Thrives in acidic PA soils. Provide plenty of organic mulch to keep roots cool and moist.

4. Groundcover, Roots & Perennial Veggies

  • Wild Strawberry: Spreads rapidly as a living green mulch with intensely sweet, tiny berries.
  • Jerusalem Artichoke (Sunchokes): Aggressive native sunflowers producing crisp, edible tubers. Tip: Plant in a dedicated corner so they don't overrun everything.
  • Ostrich Fern: Produces edible spring "fiddleheads" in damp, shady pockets.
  • Ramps (Wild Leeks): Garlic/onion-flavored woodland greens that pop up early spring under heavy shade.
  • Asparagus & Rhubarb: Hardy perennial staples that yield food every single spring with almost zero maintenance.

📅 Seasonal Planting Timeline (PA Zone 6/7)

🍂 Fall (Sept - Nov)

The Golden Window: The absolute best time to plant trees, shrubs, and perennial crowns (asparagus/rhubarb). Warm soil and cool air allow deep root establishment before winter freeze.

🌱 Spring (April - May)

Direct Sowing & Bulbs: Plant bare-root trees if missed in fall, sow cool-weather greens, and plant hardneck garlic cloves or potato tubers as the soil thaws.

☀️ Summer (June - August)

Maintenance & Prep: Do not plant new trees now (the heat stresses them out). Focus on thick wood-chip mulching around existing plants to lock in moisture against summer dry spells.
